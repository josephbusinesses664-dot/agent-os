# Positioning

## Purpose
Define the position a product owns in the customer's mind and the messaging
spine that expresses it consistently.

## Process
1. **Market** — who is the category, who are the players?
2. **Audience** — the specific buyer/user and their frame of reference.
3. **Frame of reference** — what category does the customer slot us into?
4. **Point of difference** — the benefit competitors can't claim; prove it.
5. **Reasons to believe** — evidence: metrics, case studies, architecture, team.

## Positioning statement template
"For <target customer>, who <job or need>, <product> is a <category> that
<point of difference>. Unlike <alternatives>, we <proof>."

## Differentiation test
- Would the customer notice if this claim disappeared? (if no → not a difference)
- Can a competitor copy it this quarter? (if yes → it's a feature, not a moat)

## Rules
- One position per product; messaging must be consistent across channels.
- Ground claims in what the product actually does — no invented proof.
